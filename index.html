v4 · A simulation of Meckler's six-stage dynamic symmetrical exchange model · JPIM RF 2026

Who learns fast enough to close the deal?

Four focal firms each trade with a stakeholder in the same market. Both sides build technical (T), emotional (E) and transaction-cost (TC) utility on S-curves, value what they receive against their own floors, and learn from every round. Floors are deal-breakers, and they rise. The installed base and complements grow the same way for everyone; firms can differ in allocation, learning, attention, noise and TC capability. Survived: still trading at the end (not out of resources, not terminated). Reinnovation cycles: periods with no deal, after which the firm re-runs Stages 1–6. How the model works

One period is one pass through the six stages, with the Stage 4–6 negotiation loop running inside the period. All four firms face identical copies of the same stakeholder and the same shared environment, so differences come from firm policy and the individual overrides in Firms. The paper supplies the architecture; numeric defaults are illustrative assumptions that you can change.

S-curves. Each dimension's utility is a logistic function of a capability stock: u(x) = L·(σ(k(x−m)) − σ(−km)) / (1 − σ(−km)). By default T has the highest ceiling and the slowest take-off; E and TC are cheaper but cap lower. Stocks depreciate by the capability depreciation setting each period; E decays faster by the E decay setting. Each T, E and TC curve has adjustable steepness (k), midpoint (m) and ceiling (L) in Advanced. The stakeholder uses the E and TC shapes with separate advocacy and cooperation scales. Starting stocks and all initial beliefs are adjustable there too.

Stage 1. A firm spends up to its per-period budget. It first repairs any dimension it believes is below the stakeholder's floor, because a floor breach is a deal-breaker, splitting its budget across deficient dimensions. Repair takes priority over triage and policy; if no repair is needed, a bank below the triage level funds only T. Otherwise the policy chooses the allocation. Technical-first puts the rest on T. Balanced splits it three ways. Slope-hunter ranks curves by believed weight × slope, skips any dimension it believes is at the Maximum Demanded level, and invests only when that clears the adjustable slope-hunter hurdle; otherwise it saves. Orchestrated uses the same rule with full attention, half the signal noise, full attention to the network, and an AI multiplier on its TC curve. The Firms section can override learning, attention, network attention, noise and TC steepness separately for any firm. Turning learning off freezes all of that firm’s beliefs; the stakeholder still learns. Switching the main dimension loses a share of that period's capability gain (pivot friction). When planning, firms see the network's contribution only in proportion to their network attention.

Stage 2. Installed base and complements grow on logistic paths with the same size, speed and midpoint for every firm. Separate co-evolution increments add to installed base and complements after each formalized exchange; installed base has an adjustable cap. Complements add to T; the installed base adds to TC and, with the segment's valence, to E (negative for exclusivity segments). Exogenous friction subtracts from TC.

Stage 3. The stakeholder's floors rise each period (capped at the configured share of demand ceilings). Utility above the Maximum Demanded level adds nothing. Human: perceives each dimension with noise, rejects outright if TC is below the friction-abort level, enforces every floor, then requires total value to clear the sum of floors. AI persona: no noise and no abort; it enforces only the AI persona screening share of each floor (0 by default, so it trades off only; evidence that LLMs sometimes choose lexicographically argues for testing higher values); total value must still clear the sum of market floors. Delegated agent: hard floors on T and TC at the adjustable agent floor multiplier, with an adjustable emotional weight, no E floor. Commodity buyer: weights TC heavily and E at zero (the paper's purchasing-algorithm example). Operant resources scale how much T the stakeholder can extract. Value-in-context shocks multiply the three weights for every firm by the Advanced context multipliers for the configured duration. Market floor and demand ranges, the floor cap and all evaluator weights are adjustable in Advanced.

Stage 4. The stakeholder's dependence is V / (V + alternatives). It opens by paying its believed version of the firm's payment floor plus a share of its remaining surplus that grows with dependence, never more than its adjustable maximum share of the value it assigned (converted to resources at the value-to-resource rate), and never more than its bank. It also invests in advocacy and cooperation when it believes they are below the firm's floors. Repair costs and capability gains, discretionary advocacy, initial bank and floor beliefs, and its probing behavior are adjustable in Advanced.

Stage 5. Complements augment the cooperation the firm receives (payment rails). The firm reads a noisy signal of the stakeholder's floors, ceilings and weights on the dimensions it attends to; learning-enabled firms also receive a broken-floor signal. Advanced controls set noise on those signals, failure-belief adjustments, and the stock tie-break weight in attention ranking. Operant resources affect both planning and actual technical utility.

Stage 6. The firm's payment, advocacy and cooperation floors each rise at their own adjustable multiple of the common rise. Great clears all three floors and the adjustable great-payment multiple; OK clears all three floors; not good enough triggers renegotiation: the firm states its shortfall, the stakeholder raises payment, advocacy or cooperation if its value limit and remaining bank allow, and both update their beliefs. If the rounds run out, or the stakeholder rejected in Stage 3, the period ends with no exchange and the firm reinnovates next period. After more consecutive no-deal periods than its reinnovation tolerance, the firm terminates. Every repair reduces the remaining bank available for payment, and every floor must clear before either side transfers payment. A formalized exchange pays the firm the payment plus a conversion share of the advocacy and cooperation it received, and adds the configured co-evolution increments to both shared network paths.

Settings and experiments. Every numeric model assumption has a control and a row in the complete control reference. Advanced starts collapsed. Firm overrides use −1 for shared attention settings and 0 for the default TC multiplier. Non-default settings, evaluator, illustrated firm and seed are saved in the URL; JSON import replaces all settings and restores omitted keys to defaults. Reset restores the original seed and all default controls. The summary uses 50–1000 fixed-seed markets. Sweep varies one numeric control across 5–11 points, using the same markets at each point, and runs only on a button click. Discrete controls can repeat values after rounding to their step size. Survival is the share alive at the end; formalized share is successful exchanges divided by active periods; wasted spend is spend on dimensions already at Maximum Demanded, divided by total spend; final bank is averaged across all markets, including exits.

Limits. One stakeholder segment per market, no competition between the focal firms for the same stakeholder, and stylized functional forms. It shows how the model's stated logic behaves under different settings. It does not test the theory.
