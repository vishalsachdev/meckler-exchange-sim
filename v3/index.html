Does the economizing pivot pay?

Four firms build technical (T), emotional (E) and transaction-cost (TC) utility on S-curves and sell to the same stakeholder segment. That segment has a Minimum Necessary and a Maximum Demanded threshold on each dimension. The firms differ only in how they allocate resources and how much of the market signal they attend to. Switch who evaluates the offer (a human, an AI persona simulating a human, or an AI agent buying for someone) and watch which firm survives.

How the model works

S-curves (Stage 1). Each dimension's utility is a logistic function of the capability stock: u(x) = L·(σ(k(x−m)) − σ(−km)) / (1 − σ(−km)). T has the highest ceiling and the slowest take-off; E and TC are cheaper but cap lower. Stocks depreciate 3% per period.

Thresholds (Stage 3). Each random market draws a Minimum Necessary and a Maximum Demanded level per dimension. Utility above the maximum adds no value. Four buyer types evaluate the offer. Human: screens on soft, noisy floors (each about 70% of the minimum), then requires the total to clear the sum of the minimums. AI persona: fully compensatory and noise-free; only the total counts. Delegated agent: buys for a principal against hard floors on T and TC at 110% of the minimum, and weights emotional utility at 0.2 in both value and price. Pure floors: the reference conjunctive rule, every dimension at its exact minimum.

Exchange and replenishment (Stages 4–6). A formalized exchange returns 1.6 × assigned value to the resource bank. A failed one returns nothing. A firm whose bank reaches zero exits.

Learning (Stages 5–6). Firms start out believing the market wants maximum technical utility. Each period they update three beliefs from a noisy signal, but only on the dimensions they attend to: the floor the buyer actually enforces, the buyer's Maximum Demanded level, and how much the buyer weights that dimension. The slope-hunter and the orchestrated firm rank curves by believed weight × slope, and invest only when that clears a break-even hurdle (about 0.08, from the sale price, 3% depreciation and a 10% discount rate). Otherwise they save. Technical-only and balanced firms do not learn. The last buyer option switches from humans to delegated agents halfway through the run.

Policies. Technical-only spends one unit per period on T. Balanced splits one unit three ways. Slope-hunter follows the paper: it fixes any dimension it believes is below minimum, then puts everything on the steepest curve still below its believed maximum, and saves when none is. Orchestrated uses the same rule but reads signals on all three dimensions with half the noise, and AI steepens its TC curve.

Limits. One segment, no competition between firms, no negotiation loop, no network effects. It shows how the model's logic behaves. It does not test the theory.
